Testing

What to know about the SAT, ACT, PSAT, and AP exams.

SAT

A core part of most college applications. Start with a practice test to see where you stand, then focus your studying on your weakest section instead of reviewing everything equally.

ACT

Covers similar ground to the SAT but includes a science section and moves faster. Take a practice test for each to see which format fits you better before committing to one.

PSAT

A lower-stakes practice run for the SAT, usually taken sophomore or junior year. A strong score can also qualify you for National Merit recognition, so it's worth taking seriously.

APs

Give you a shot at college credit and show colleges you can handle rigor. Pick ones tied to your interests or strengths, and pace your studying across the year instead of cramming before the exam.

How to Register

SAT

Use the College Board website to register for the SAT. Make sure to use your personal email for convenience when it comes to sending scores (often, school accounts get deleted after senior year).

ACT

Create a MyACT account on this link and use it to register. Just like for the SAT, use your personal email for this.

PSAT

This is usually done with your school towards the start of junior year. Reach out to counselors or even upperclassmen to get information on the process.

APs

To register for APs, you'll typically need to work with your school (either taking a course and exam or just an exam). If not, you can also find test centers near you and check with them. This varies from place to place; consider asking your counselor or searching up centers and contacting them directly.

ACT

PSAT

  • Khan Academy – About the PSAT/NMSQT
  • Mometrix – PSAT practice test

APs

There are many different AP courses, and so there's no one good place to study for them. Instead, find good resources for each individual exam by searching up what others have used. Often, YouTube courses and online books are a good bet, but do your research!
